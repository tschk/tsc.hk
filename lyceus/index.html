Lyceus is a research project developing an independently initialized transformer family for personal agents. It explores learning from feedback, adaptation to people and domains, model memory, and growth in weights and capacity.

A research project by The Software Company of Hong Kong.

Memory beyond the conversation

Personal memory could live in model-associated state, rather than relying only on chat history or search results pasted into each request. A user-controlled profile could travel between compatible model generations, making a personal experience easier to carry forward.

A profile is information a person or host chooses to provide. Learned memory is different: changes to model-associated state or weights through experience. Research prototypes accept optional personal and domain state supplied by a host; reliable learned memory and profile portability remain research goals.

How it is intended to work

Research prototype

The model holds representations and weights; personal and domain state are intended to remain distinct from reusable competence. Its host controls permissions and coordinates observations, tools, evaluation and checkpoints. Retrieval supplies context; learning changes the model.

Scripted synthetic updates have been tested; persistent learning, reliable retention and autonomous adaptation remain research objectives.

Model sizes

Small prototypes and a roughly 102-million-parameter prototype have completed training experiments. A configurable family and larger models are under investigation, not released products.
