Lyceus is a research project developing an independently initialized transformer family for personal agents. It explores learning from feedback, adaptation to people and domains, model memory, and growth in weights and capacity.

A research project by The Software Company of Hong Kong.

How the parts fit together

Research prototype

The model holds representations and weights; personal and domain state are intended to remain distinct from reusable competence. Its host controls permissions and coordinates observations, tools, evaluation and checkpoints. Retrieval supplies context; learning changes the model.

Scripted synthetic updates have been tested; persistent learning, reliable retention and autonomous adaptation remain research objectives.

Areas of research

Memory

Memory in model-associated state could carry learning beyond a single conversation. Prototypes accept optional host-supplied personal and domain state; reliable learned memory remains under investigation.

Adaptation

Feedback can inform candidate updates to weights or state. Scripted synthetic experiments exercise updates; reliable retention and autonomous adaptation are not yet demonstrated.

Growth

Experiments explore adding model capacity as well as changing existing parameters. Useful growth that preserves earlier learning remains research.

Profiles

A profile is information a person or host chooses to provide, distinct from learned memory. Carrying a profile between compatible model generations is an open research direction.

Model family

Small prototypes and a roughly 102-million-parameter prototype have completed training experiments. A configurable family and larger models are under investigation, not released products.

Harness

A separate host is intended to manage permissions, tools and evidence, and assess candidate changes before promoting or rolling back checkpoints.
