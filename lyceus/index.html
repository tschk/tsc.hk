Lyceus is a scratch-trained native model research project for personal agents. Its current direction combines text generation, persistent decision state and efficient sequence processing in one coordinated system.

A research project by The Software Company of Hong Kong.

How the parts fit together

Research prototype

The proposed text-only design brings a text denoiser and a decision component together inside one agent. Across refinement rounds, the decision state carries information forward and helps decide what to reveal, revisit or continue. Personal state has a separate lifetime; the host retains control of permissions, evaluation and checkpoints.

The coupling and learned decisions are proposed, not yet trained. Generation state is distinct from durable personal knowledge.

Areas of research

Memory

Memory in model-associated state could carry learning beyond a single conversation. Prototypes accept optional host-supplied personal and domain state; reliable learned memory remains under investigation.

Adaptation

Feedback can inform candidate updates to weights or state. Scripted synthetic experiments exercise updates; reliable retention and autonomous adaptation are not yet demonstrated.

Growth

Research explores models directing changes to their own parameters and capacity, and, at larger sizes, proposing transformer architecture or source changes for independent review. Growth is currently host-scheduled; model-directed structural and source changes are not demonstrated.

Profiles

A research interface accepts host-selected personal and domain state for individual input rows while reusing the same loaded model. This synthetic prototype is distinct from learned memory; it is not a multi-user service or a guarantee of concurrent isolation.

Model family

Small prototypes and a roughly 102-million-parameter prototype have completed training experiments. A configurable family and larger models are under investigation, not released products.

Harness

A separate host is intended to manage permissions, tools and evidence, and assess candidate changes before promoting or rolling back checkpoints.

Research in progress

Tested

Native text and decision experiments, synthetic state updates and scheduled growth have been exercised separately. An FFT convolution operator passed CPU and MPS output and gradient checks—not a whole-model performance test.

Investigating

Text diffusion, decision-state coupling and subquadratic sequence processing are being studied together. Corrected source text and bounded larger-model resource screens inform the next comparison.

Still open

Language samples remain incoherent. Reliable personal corrections, retained knowledge, controlled adaptation and useful model-directed growth are not established.
